The TV Revolution

The Era of Saturday Morning Cartoons

The Rise of Limited Animation

Beginning in the early 1950's through the mid-1960's television sets became a common household item. With the advent of television, there was an increase in demand for animation. Since, at this point, many of the studios who produced animation for film (Disney and Warner Brothers) had high costs and long production times associated with producing animated films. Therefore, the development of "Limited Animation" occurred. Studios such as Hanna-Barbera developed Limited Animation by creating movement in only specific areas of a character (such as mouth movements or eye movements) while leaving other areas of the body stationary. Some may recall seeing cartoon characters wearing collars or ties because these items would help mask the separation of the head from the body so that when an animator needed to replace one of a series of identical-looking heads on a single body they could do so quickly and efficiently.

While some people felt that the use of Limited Animation was a retrograde step in terms of artistic quality, it did provide a platform for a tremendous amount of creative freedom in the way stories were told. During the 1960's we saw Superhero Epics, Space Age Comedies (*The Jetsons*) and Mystery Solving Adventures (*Scooby Doo*) to name but a few examples. What used to be cartoons that preceded movies and lasted anywhere from two to five minutes now became 30 minute television programs that shaped the cultural identity of a generation of young viewers.

The 1980s and the Power of Merchandising

By the 1980's the world of cartoons had changed once again. Television deregulations related to commercial advertising provided opportunities for producers to create cartoons specifically designed to serve as 22-minute commercials for toy lines. Cartoon shows such as *He-Man & the Masters of the Universe*, *Transformers*, and *G.I. Joe* were ubiquitous on television. These cartoon shows were created based upon financial needs rather than artistic needs. The "90s Renaissance" is the culmination of this time period. Television animation became "cinematic", "mood-oriented", and "adult" as seen with series such as *Batman: The Animated Series*. At the same time, Nickelodeon and Cartoon Network began creating original "creator-driven" content like *Ren & Stimpy* and *Dexter's Laboratory* where the creative vision of the animator would come before the sale of toys based on characters. This time bridge the gap between limited animation of the past and the digital mastery of the future by showing that the small screen can also be a platform for high art animation.
