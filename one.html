The Early Days

Exploring the History of Cartoons

STARTING POINT

The concept of "hand-drawn" cartoons had existed since the early 1900s; however, "hand-drawn" cartoons did not have a story line until the 1920s. Gertie the Dinosaur (Winsor McCay, 1914) is credited with being the first cartoon character to be given a distinct personality. For each drawing of this cartoon, Winsor McCay drew thousands of drawings. This laborious work continued to be done by hand throughout the decade. Eventually, the "Rubber Hose" style came into use. Character movements were restricted due to their lack of elbows and knees and they moved like rubber hoses. Early versions of Mickey Mouse and Popeye cartoons used this style because it provided fluidity and exaggeration beyond physics.

The reason we believe cartoons are "alive," is based on Persistence of Vision. It is the condition when an object remains on your retinas for a small portion of time after it has left your sight. Traditional hand-drawn cartoons require 24 separate frames per second. The space between these frames allows us to fill in the missing images to create smooth motion. A six minute cartoon would require nearly nine thousand separate drawings. Because of the extreme amount of labor needed to draw all those images, the studio created cells, which were transparent sheets of celluloid. Cels were painted over static backgrounds allowing artists to avoid having to redraw a large part of a scene for every frame.

Continuance

Beginning in the 1930s through the 1980s, cartoons became a major force in entertainment. Major studios such as Disney and Warner Bros. produced many classic cartoons. Both Disney and Warner Bros. contributed significantly to the world of cartoons during the Golden Age. Disney's focus was developing what he called "the illusion of life." He sought to make his animated characters appear as realistic as possible. Warner Bros. developed a new type of cartoon using humor and absurdity. Their Looney Tunes series included characters such as Bugs Bunny and Daffy Duck. Each of these types of cartoons represented opposing philosophies regarding how animation should be made. As such, both Disney and Warner Bros. styles of cartoon making dominated the field of animation for approximately 50 years. Cartoons proved that there was much more than simply childlike entertainment available within animation.
